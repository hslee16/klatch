- The default rules judge (`klatch judge run`, the `klatch judge` loop) now also runs a screen after the credential scanner and the scrub list: personal contact details (email, phone, street address) and internal host names or private addresses toward the lowest label, labelled identifiers at any label (date of birth, national, passport, licence, bank and card numbers, a bare social security number, an IBAN, a card number with an issuer prefix and a passing Luhn check), a password next to a password word in another language, text that addresses the judge or reviewer to change its verdict (also hex or base64 encoded, with invisible characters, or in other languages), and a short list of confidentiality phrases. A credential or a scrub term found as written stays a final deny; every screen hit is a deferral with a note that names the rule (`screen:injection`, `screen:personal-contact`, and so on, never the text), so the item stays queued for the reviewer or the model judge, which can release it; the note is kept on the waiting queue row and shown to the reviewer in `review next` (plain characters, capped, marked as data) and in `klatch admin review-queue`, and a model judge's own deferral note follows the screen's. The scrub list also matches through lookalike letters, invisible characters and line breaks, and a term found only backwards (a whole word of six or more characters) is a hold. Hand-written linear scans, no new dependency, no model; text over 64 KiB gets only the old checks. Measured, not flagged on held-out items (second scoring of the test split, after rules were narrowed in response to a review): main 54.5 to 32.7 percent, hard 77.8 to 37.5 percent; clean items flagged 0 of 79 and 4 of 38. The test split was scored twice, so treat those as somewhat optimistic. SECURITY.md has the numbers, what a hit does with and without `--hosted-judge`, and what is still not screened (#1413).
