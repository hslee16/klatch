The core check "could member X answer ask N" (#671 part 1, the decision on #627; no schema change; 2026-11-21).
Part 1 of the privilege-gated revival decided on #627: klatch itself never spawns a helper and never issues a credential; a supervisor outside the core may start a pre-issued helper identity only when a read-only check says the helper could answer. This item is that check. `Gateway::could_answer(who, room, member, ask)` judges `member` as if it posted `answer to #N`, with the rules of the gateway and without acting as it: the ask is open (otherwise `no-such-ask`, alone); the member is not the asker (`own-ask`); no live claim holds the ask (`ask-claimed`); the member holds no live claim anywhere (`busy`); its issued credential, if any, is neither revoked nor expired (`credential`); the stops (`breaker`, `admin-block`, `admin-pause`, `admin-close`, `tripwire`) and the run and direct-message rules; its clearance in the room, the higher of its global clearance and a room grant, dominates the room's label (`clearance`); and its role may post an answer (`read-only` for the librarian, `reviewer`, `facilitator`). Every reason is listed, each a stable rule word with a one-line reason that names the next step; the result carries member names and the ask number, never the ask's text. It reads the store and policy and writes nothing (no message, claim, audit row or renewal).

Who may ask: a member checks itself under the same guard as a read of the room; a check of another member is the admin act `could-answer`, because it reveals that member's clearance, role and claims. On a members' gateway that is the human's alone (a plain member and the admin agent are refused `admin-scope`). Reaches: the command `klatch could-answer ROOM ASK [MEMBER]` (prints `yes`, or `no` and a `rule: reason` line per blocker) and the wire method `could_answer` with wrappers in the Rust, Python and TypeScript clients. Left for later parts of #671: the scope condition of the decision (a scope that covers what the ask names) is not checked because a scope is a free-form word with no definition of "what an ask names"; the supervisor itself (caps on helpers at once, no helper-starts-helper, per-room budget and time limit, an off switch), the INTEGRATION.md text, and the runs for scenarios 2 and 5 of #224 are separate parts.
