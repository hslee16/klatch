Revocation of client certificates by a CRL file on the mutual TLS listener (#1491, refs #108, #157; no schema change; 2026-11-25).
`klatch-server https ... --tls-client-ca PEM [--tls-client-crl PEM]`: the optional file holds one or more X.509 CRLs, signed by the CA of the client CA file, read once at start. It is handed to the same rustls `WebPkiClientVerifier` that `tls.rs` already builds (`with_crls`), so there is one verifier and one listener and the CRL covers every route behind the handshake: HTTP, the event stream, MCP and the WebSocket route. The `klatch` binary is untouched and links no TLS; no new dependency, feature flag or unsafe code.

    What a revoked client sees. A certificate whose serial the CRL of its issuer lists fails the handshake: the client gets a fatal TLS alert (certificate revoked; with TLS 1.3 the client may finish its own side first and learn of it on its first read) and no byte of HTTP. No session exists and no connection slot is held beyond the handshake deadline. The server logs `client certificate refused: revoked (listed by the client CRL)`, at most one such line every ten seconds, with no subject or serial.

    The file. Refused at start, with a message that names the file and the failure (never its content), before the store is opened: missing or unreadable, a symbolic link, not a regular file, writable by group or other (whoever can write it can un-revoke a certificate), empty, not valid PEM, no CRL in it, or a CRL rustls cannot parse. The checks mirror the key file's (opened with `O_NOFOLLOW` and `O_NONBLOCK`, checked on the open file); there is no owner check, because a CRL is public and a root-owned file is normal. `--tls-client-crl` without `--tls-client-ca`, or given twice, is refused in words.

    One CRL per issuer. rustls-webpki uses the first CRL it finds for an issuer, so `cat new.crl >> client.crl` would keep an older (even empty) list in force and ignore the new revocations, or keep an expired one in force and refuse every client. The server reads the issuer name of each CRL with a small hand-written DER reader (`issuer_of` in `tls_crl.rs`: definite minimal lengths of at most four bytes, no recursion, nothing allocated, so no input makes it slow or large; no dependency) and refuses at start a file in which two CRLs share an issuer: replace the file, never append. The same reader is the first half of the check #1614 asks for. The file is also capped at 4 MiB (checked before it is read, and the read is bounded again), at 16 CRLs, and must be owned by the server's user or root.

    Silent lock-outs, now loud. A CRL with a bad signature, an unsupported signature algorithm, or an issuer certificate whose key usage lacks cRLSign makes the verifier fail every certificate of that issuer. Each of those is `InvalidCertRevocationList` in rustls and now has its own log line (a fixed text, no subject or serial, its own rate-limit slot) next to the revoked and expired ones. The unknown-status slot was removed: with unknown status allowed it cannot happen.

    Expiry: enforced (`enforce_revocation_expiration`). When a CRL passes its `nextUpdate`, every client certificate its issuer signed is refused, because a stale list proves nothing. The alternative (ignore expiry) would keep a revoked certificate working for as long as nobody renewed the list, which is the silent failure this option exists to end. So the lock-out is loud: the start line says that expiry is enforced and what to do, and each refusal logs `the CRL has expired ... replace the CRL file and restart the server` (once per ten seconds). The fix is a fresh file and a SIGHUP (reload, #1492) or a restart.

    Unknown status: allowed (`UnknownStatusPolicy::Allow`, rustls `allow_unknown_revocation_status`). rustls's other setting, deny, refuses any certificate in the chain that no configured CRL covers, which includes an intermediate CA when the operator lists only the CRL of the CA that issues clients (checked up to, not including, the trust anchor). Allow gives the rule the ticket asked for: a certificate is denied when a CRL of its issuer is configured and lists it (or is expired), and is otherwise accepted as without the option. Cost, stated: a CRL signed by the wrong CA would do nothing and nothing in rustls says so (answered by the start-up checks of #1614, below).

    Tests. `tests/tls_crl.rs` builds certificates and CRLs with rcgen relative to now (no literal dates): a revoked certificate is refused on five routes and sees the revoked alert while an unlisted one connects; an expired CRL refuses a certificate it does not list (the test fails without `enforce_revocation_expiration`) and the log says why; a CRL by another CA does not apply (the documented hazard); nine kinds of bad file stop the server before it listens, as do a file over 4 MiB, one with 17 CRLs and one with two CRLs of an issuer (while CRLs of different issuers may share a file); a forged CRL and an intermediate CA without cRLSign each lock their clients out and log it, and an intermediate that may sign CRLs revokes like a root; thirty revoked handshakes make at most two log lines (the test fails with the limit at zero); `issuer_of` is tested on truncation at every length, lying, indefinite and non-minimal lengths and deep nesting; the option needs a CA; the start line. `tests/no_network.rs` pins that exactly one place builds the client verifier and that it sets `with_crls` and expiry enforcement.

    Not covered. The owner check has a unit test of its rule only (a file owned by another user cannot be made by a test that is not root). OCSP and CRL download (the server makes no network request for any of it). Delta CRLs and CRL scoping beyond what rustls-webpki does. 

    Start-up checks of #1614 (2026-11-25; no schema change, no new dependency; revised after the independent review). Owner decision: a hand-written bounded DER reader, not a direct rustls-webpki dependency. It lives in `src/der.rs` (the issuer reader moved there from `tls_crl.rs`, with `next_update_of` and a strict `time_to_unix`). At start, `read_crls` also takes the subjects of the client CA file (the verifier's trust anchors, whose subject is the DER content of the Name that webpki compares with a CRL's issuer, so the file is read once) and the clock reading `now_unix()`; the pure `check_cas_and_times` takes `now`, so tests never touch the wall clock.

    Refused: a CRL whose issuer or times cannot be read; a CRL with no `nextUpdate` (optional in X.509, but rustls-webpki refuses such a CRL with a parse error, so the server refuses it at start in words instead of printing a warning and then failing); a `thisUpdate` or `nextUpdate` before 1970 (webpki's floor); a system clock before 1970. Warned, never refused (fixed lines naming the file and the CRL's number, never a subject or a serial): `nextUpdate` now or past (expiry is enforced); within 7 days (`SOON_SECS`); and a CRL whose issuer matches no CA subject of the client CA file. That last one is a warning because webpki applies a CRL to every certificate of the chain whose issuer name matches, so the CRL of an intermediate CA that issues the clients works with a root-only CA file (the verified #1617 setup); it does nothing only for a CRL no certificate of any chain was issued by. The line says so. Listing an intermediate in the CA file to silence it is not advised: it becomes a trust anchor, whose validity and key usages webpki does not check and whose revocation by the root no longer applies. (An optional intermediates file, certificates used only to match CRL issuers and never anchors, is #1623.)

    Time rules (`der.rs`): UTCTime is exactly 13 bytes `YYMMDDHHMMSSZ`, YY of 50 or more is 19YY and below is 20YY (RFC 5280); GeneralizedTime exactly 15 bytes `YYYYMMDDHHMMSSZ`; `Z` required (no offset, no lower case), no fractional seconds, digits only, month 1 to 12, day within the month (leap years), hour under 24, minute and second under 60 (no leap second); then a result before 1970 is refused as such. GeneralizedTime before 2050 is accepted though RFC 5280 asks for UTCTime.

    Tests. `tests/tls_crl_issuer.rs`: a stranger CRL starts with the warning (no subject, no serial); a matching one starts silently; an intermediate CRL with a root-only CA file starts, warns and revokes its leaf; the root's CRL revoking the intermediate still refuses its clients; an intermediate-only CA file works; past, near and far `nextUpdate` (warns, warns, silent) with relative dates; each CRL judged alone and numbered; sixteen distinct issuers accepted (#1622); a CRL with its nextUpdate cut out of an rcgen CRL is refused at start in words; a CRL with times before 1970 is refused. `tests/tls_crl.rs` is as #1617 left it (root-only intermediate set-up, the test that an intermediate without cRLSign locks its clients out is back, #1624) apart from the 5 MiB message. `src/der_tests.rs`: valid times to the second, the year pivot at 50, 35 malformed encodings, 1970 floor, truncation at every length, lying, indefinite and non-minimal lengths, deep nesting, and a deterministic 6000-case random run asserting no panic. `src/tls_crl.rs` unit tests: the 7-day edges, whole-name matching, the missing-nextUpdate and before-1970 refusals, malformed time refusal. The 5 MiB sparse file is told from the bounded read (two messages).

    Not covered. Periodic re-checks (reload is by SIGHUP only, item 1492).
