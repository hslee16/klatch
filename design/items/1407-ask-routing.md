Ask routing: an ask finds its own helper (#1407, epic #1400; schema 44; 2026-11-23). In the dogfood review 0 of 519 lobby messages were asks, and the real build relied on the human to route work: asks had `could_answer` (#671, #1171), the wake (#349) and stuck detection, and nothing connected an ask to the member who can take it. When an ask is posted the gateway now computes, by rules only, the members that could take it, addresses the ask to them, and addresses it to the coordinators when nobody takes it within the lease. Metric it serves: time to first answer and unanswered asks in S4 (#1369); the offline measure is a fixture (`gateway/tests/ask_routing.rs`, `steps_from_the_ask_to_the_capable_member_seeing_it_with_and_without_routing`), in scripted steps of 30 seconds (one hook turn each) from the post of the ask to the capable member seeing it in its digest: a member that reads everything sees it at step 1 with and without routing (the digest already ranked an ask it could answer first, #1405, so routing changes nothing for it); a member that narrowed its delivery view to its own scope sees it at step 1 with routing and never within ten steps without; a busy coordinator that muted the room sees it at step 3 (the 90 second lease) with routing and never without.

    Who is routed to, and by which rules. For each member of the room other than the asker, the gateway asks the same check `could_answer` runs for an answer (the guard on who asks is skipped, since the gateway is the one asking; `could_answer_of` is the shared body): the ask is open, it is not the member's own, the declared scopes of ask and member do not differ (#1171), no claim holds the ask, the member holds no claim anywhere (busy), its credential is live, it is not stopped (breaker, removed from the room, paused, closed or tripped room, run and direct message rules), its clearance in the room dominates the room's label, and its role may post. A member that fails any of these is not addressed, so a member whose clearance does not cover a sealed label is never routed to and never learns of the ask through routing (tested with a label above the member's clearance, and a room grant counts as it does for `could_answer`). The members that pass are ordered by an affinity computed from structured fields only: 2 when the ask and the member declare the same scope, 2 for a `repo` ref equal to the member's scope, 2 for a `path` ref the member itself edited (the trail of #1408, the `:N` line suffix dropped), 1 for a `path` ref in a directory it edited in; then the most recently seen; then the name. At most three are addressed (`ROUTE_MAX`): more would be chatter, and three leaves a second and a third if the first is gone. No word of a message is read, so no keyword can steer an ask. The status machine class the ticket names is not an input: klatch has no per-member machine class in the store to read, so it is left out rather than guessed. A direct message is never routed (an ask there is already addressed to the other member).

    What addressing does. The record is `ask_routes(room, seq, member, kind, ts, told)`: names and numbers, never text, and not a message post. The digest ranks an open unclaimed ask addressed to the reader as class Ask (`importances`, the same class an ask mentioning the reader has), lists it among the `addressed` messages the hook never drops for the byte budget, and lets it through a narrowed delivery view (`split_view`) and a mute (`digest_messages`). The wake count (`wake_summary`) counts it for a member that muted the room, so a member that opted into wake (`init --wake`, the `klatch wake` watcher) is woken by it; a member that reads everything was already woken by the message itself. An ask that is claimed (taken) or answered or closed stops being addressed: `routed_asks` reads only open unclaimed asks.

    Escalation. The lease is the room's ask lease (90 seconds, 10 to 300 by `room-ask-lease`, #242). An ask routed and not claimed after the lease, or one that was taken and is no longer claimed while still open (the lease ran out or the claim was released: klatch cannot tell the two apart from the claim rows alone, and either way the ask needs a helper), is addressed once to the members of the room that hold the role admin agent or integrator (the roles `roles.rs` and the priority classes of #1405 call coordinating) and that pass `could_answer` except `busy` and `scope` (a coordinator is expected to be busy, and need not share the scope; the label, the stops and the role still refuse it). An ask that no one could take at all (`routed to nobody`) escalates the same way. The escalation is a routing record and an event, not a post and not a new `stuck` event kind: the existing `stuck` listing is unchanged (its `nobody_capable` event is about members that declined with `cannot`, a different case), but `stuck` runs the sweep. By then the coordinator's read, hook and wake cursors are past the ask message, so the late addressing reaches it another way: the digest lists the ask once with the session start backlog lines and the wake count includes it once (`told` bits 1 and 2 remember each surface). A coordinator already addressed as capable is not told again. With no coordinator in the room the escalation is recorded with a count of zero and nothing else happens: no coordinator is created.

    Where it runs, and why. In the gateway, at post time and lazily afterwards, and not in the mediator process (`klatch mediator`, #271). The mediator is a separate loop that turns stuck events into proposals; routing has to work in a room that runs no such process, must be cheap and deterministic, and has to be in place before the first digest of the member it addresses, which a loop polling afterwards cannot promise. Everything it reads is already in the gateway's store, so no new process, socket, timer or network exists. Posting hooks: `post_labeled`, `post_handoff` and `post_ask` (after the scope and refs are stored, which are part of what routing reads). The lease is checked, with no timer, when a digest, a wake count, a `stuck` listing or a claim on an `ask:` path next looks at the room, each running the one cheap sweep (one query when the room never routed). A sweep or a routing failure is dropped and never fails the post, read or digest that triggered it. The asks of an upgraded store posted before this schema are never routed or escalated (the tables start empty).

    What it never does. It posts nothing, claims nothing, answers nothing and closes nothing on a member's behalf, holds no admin act (nothing in it calls `administer`), reads no message text, and addresses no member that cannot read the room. The human sees it in `klatch admin stats` and stops it with the policy key `room:ROOM:ask-routing` set to `off` (read at every use); there is no admin act or command to set the key yet, which is ticketed (#1460), so for now it is set the way other policy keys are in a test or by hand in the store. The routing never reaches the model of a member except through the same digest line any addressed message has.

    Counts and retention. `klatch admin stats` prints one line, `ask routing: N routed to capable members, M routed to nobody, T taken, L lapsed, E escalated (U with no coordinator to reach)`, and the JSON the same, from `ask_route_events(room, seq, outcome, n, ts)`, one row per ask and outcome (`routed`, `taken`, `lapsed`, `escalated`), noted once each. Rows older than 30 days are deleted as new ones are written, and a member's rows go with the member when it is pruned. Schema 44 (`migrate_v43`): `ask_routes`, `ask_route_events`; existing stores start empty. Tests: `gateway/tests/ask_routing.rs` (capable member picked by scope and role; an incapable, busy, removed, reviewer or librarian member not addressed; the asker never; the best three by affinity; a sealed label routes only to cleared members and escalation skips an uncleared coordinator; a taken ask stops and counts once; an answered ask stops; a never taken and a lapsed ask escalate once; no coordinator is recorded as unreached; no routing in a direct message; nothing posted and no text stored; the room switch; the digest ranks the routed ask first through a narrowed view and the same member without routing does not see it; a muted wake-opted member is woken only by a routed ask; an escalated coordinator is woken once and listed once; the steps fixture), the store tests, the migration test and the stats test. Not done: an admin act and command for the room switch (#1460), claim and ask-room nudges, the `klatch asks` listing of who an ask was addressed to (the Rust `ask_routes` is the read; no wire method or command yet), and measuring S4 (#1369) in a benchmark rerun, which needs the multi-agent runs, so the ticket stays open until they show the gain.
