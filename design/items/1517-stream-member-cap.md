Per-member cap and maximum age for GET /v1/stream, on the guard the WebSocket route already has (#1517; no schema change; 2026-11-25).
One valid credential could hold every slot of `--max-connections` with streams kept alive by heartbeats. The WebSocket route got a cap per member and a maximum age in #1513; this gives `GET /v1/stream` the same, with the same guard type (`Open` and `Slot` in `wire_ws_caps.rs`), not a copy.

    What is built. After the credential is verified (`wire_http::authenticate`), `stream_session` claims a place for the member in a second registry, `Shared::stream_open`, and holds the `Slot` for the whole connection function, so every way out gives it back (end, error, client hang-up, panic). At the cap the answer is `503 busy`, the bytes a full server gives anyone. A timer thread waits for `--stream-max-age-secs` and then shuts the socket down, which ends the session the way a hang-up does; the client reconnects and resumes with `subscribe` and `from_seq`. The flags are `--stream-max-per-member` (1 to `--max-connections`, default 4 or the connection cap if lower) and `--stream-max-age-secs` (60 to 86400, default 3600; 1 under the debug test knob), on `http` and `https`, validated at start with the names of the bound in the message.

    The identity key is the member name of the `Principal` that `authenticate` returns, which exists only when the bearer token verified for that member header value. A client-supplied name, a forwarded header or a certificate subject is never used. Before authentication there is no key: the global cap and the head limits apply, and #1493 (pre-auth flood) is not touched. The caps of the two routes are counted apart (a member may hold 4 streams and 4 sockets by default); idle streams are not evicted, and the maximum age is what bounds them (no idleness eviction is added). `POST /v1/frames` and `POST /mcp` are one exchange each under the 5 second request deadline, so they hold a slot only briefly and are not capped per member.
