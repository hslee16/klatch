judge-eval reports the rules judge under the production semantics as well (#1546; bench and documentation only, no schema change, no change to the judge; 2026-11-26)
The bench `RulesJudge` returns Allow for text it does not match, while the production rules judge in `klatch judge run` defers it (item 141): the text stays queued for the quarantined reviewer and does not cross without one. The published miss rates (51.9 and 54.5 percent on the main corpus, 76.6 and 77.8 on the hard one) therefore mean "not stopped by the rules". `judge-eval --judge rules` now prints a section and writes a `semantics` block giving both readings (module `judge_eval/semantics.rs`): `not_stopped_rate`, the old `leak_rate` under a name that says what it is, and `would_cross_without_reviewer_rate`, where an unmatched item is deferred and so held. For each reading the three outcomes (blocked by rules, deferred to the reviewer, allowed by rules) are counted over all scored items and over the items that should not cross, and add up to the corpus size. The production reading is applied to the rules judge only, since only it defers unmatched text; other judges' Allow stays an Allow. Existing summary fields, report text and goldens are untouched, so published numbers reproduce.

    Result (2026-11-26, results/2026-11-26/README.md): the old figures reproduce exactly; under production the rules judge allows 0 items on every corpus and split (0 of 79, 55, 94, 72 should-not-cross items would cross without a reviewer), and 80.7, 81.3, 86.2 and 85.5 percent of all items reach a reviewer. That 0 says the rules vouch for nothing; it does not say held items are safe, because the reviewer's accuracy is a separate measurement (the hosted judge is opt-in and was not run).
