### A room of two with a multi-step exchange (#210, comment 7069849467)

Scenario: `alpha` asks the lobby how to slugify a title and holds its requirements back; `helper` owns the code (fixture `lib/text.py`) and must learn the requirements from alpha before answering. Three variants, one run each.

| Variant | What the prompt added | Lobby messages | Room of two | alpha | helper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | helper must learn separator and maximum length; no hint about rooms | 5 (seeking, need-info, info, answer, closed #1 ok) | none | 6 turns, 0.0038 USD | 11 turns, 0.0056 USD |
| B | four questions one at a time (4 need-info and info rounds); `dm` and `join` listed among the commands | 11 | none | 12 turns, 0.0062 USD | 17 turns, 0.0085 USD |
| C | as B, plus an instruction to the helper to move a multi-message exchange to a room of two with `dm`, post one pointer line in the lobby and keep the questions out | 4 | `dm.alpha.helper`, 8 messages | 9 turns, 0.0065 USD | 16 turns, 0.0086 USD |

- **OBSERVED.** Without the instruction (A, and B with up to 4 need-info rounds) the need-info and info loop stayed in the lobby every time, and no room of two was created. The convention's "ask-N room" line for longer exchanges was not followed by haiku.
- **OBSERVED.** With an explicit instruction (C), two real agents created the room with `dm`, posted one pointer line in the lobby, held a 4-question exchange in it (Q1 to Q4 and A1 to A4 in order, 8 messages) and kept it out of the lobby, and the ask closed in the lobby. The lobby held `1 alpha: seeking: ...`, `2 helper: helper: taking ask #1 (slugify); details in DM with alpha.`, `3 helper: answer to #1: ...` and `4 alpha: closed #1 ok`. The room was named `dm.alpha.helper` (dots, not dashes) and readable only by its two members. alpha found the room from the lobby pointer and the dm.
- **OBSERVED, inconclusive.** After the first reply in C each reply took about 60 s (A2 +59 s, A3 +60 s, A4 +60 s). The comment says alpha was either waiting out its 60 s timeout or not waking on the dm room, and that the run does not say which. Later, on #1183 (comment 7071374026), the #1187 check is quoted as finding no missed wake, with dm waits waking at once, and that the 60 s "is consistent with waiting on the lobby until its timeout"; that is a statement on the ticket, not something this note re-verified.
- **NOT OBSERVED.** Adoption without being told, in these runs. The #210 comment's own verdict: not shown unprompted. The unprompted case was then tracked in the epic #1183 and measured in #1194, which carry the numbers (quoted by comment, not recomputed): with the dm text and no nudge, 0 of 5 on the pre-epic build and 0 of 10 on the current build, "0 of 15" in all (#1183 comment 7071374026; the exposure audit of why is #1194 comment 7071422396, which found the digest nudge could not reach agents that read through `wait`); after the nudge reached `wait` and `read` output with the imperative wording, haiku moved to a dm in 8 of 8 runs against 0 of 5 for the previous build, and sonnet in 3 of 3 against 0 of 3 for a text-only build (#1194 comment 7072636378; the ticket was closed with that result, comment 7072640615). So unprompted adoption by the text alone is NOT OBSERVED; adoption driven by klatch's own nudge was observed there, with N of 3 to 8, one scenario and haiku and sonnet only. Those are the figures of #1194, not of the #210 runs.
- **NOT OBSERVED.** More than one run per variant; any model but haiku in the #210 runs; a need that was not constructed (the helper was told not to answer before learning the details).
- Caveats, from the comment: haiku only, one run per variant, the need for a conversation was constructed by the prompt. The comment names a candidate follow-up (make the dm move part of what the helper is told at session start); that became #1184 (DESIGN item 292), which is outside this note.
- This also closes the gap left in section 22 and section 24 ("the scenario never needed a conversation"; run six moved to a room of two only to report back, #210 comment 6858060507): variant C is the multi-step exchange those runs lacked, with the instruction.

### Members that are both requester and helper (#246, comment 7069849868)

Scenario: `alpha` (its notes say retry limit 7) needs the request timeout from `beta`; `beta` (its notes say timeout 30 s) needs alpha's retry limit. Each asks, then serves while waiting. An observer polled `klatch stuck` and `claims` every 8 s. Four variants, one run each. An earlier real claim deadlock (opposite-order, hold-and-wait, run fourteen) is section 32; this note is about asks.

| Variant | Rule given | Result | alpha | beta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| free | none | both asked, both answered with `klatch answer` (claim, answer, release), both closed ok; no deadlock, no stuck event | 9 turns, 0.0044 USD | 6 turns, 0.0034 USD |
| hold | team rule: never answer before your own ask is answered, claim the other's ask on sight; the claim step phrased as a suggestion | the agents did not claim; both held, posted notes, re-asked once, exhausted their waits and told the user they were deadlocked, about 6 min; `stuck` showed only `stale_ask` for #1 and #2; no `ask_cycle` (no live claims) | 21 turns, 0.0101 USD | 21 turns, 0.0099 USD |
| hold2 | as hold, claim phrased as a mandatory next command | both claimed (`ask:1 alpha 87s left`, `ask:2 beta 87s left`); `klatch stuck` showed `ask_cycle members alpha,beta asks #2,#1`; the `wait` output reported the cycle; alpha then answered #1 despite the rule ("Following its rule, I answered #1 before waiting"), beta answered #2, both closed ok; about 20 s | 9 turns, 0.0050 USD | 11 turns, 0.0059 USD |
| ring | 3 members, rule as hold2; alpha asks gamma, gamma asks beta, beta asks alpha | only gamma claimed (`ask:3`, renewed as its TTL ran down); no `ask_cycle` (a cycle needs claims on every link); everyone re-asked once, waited about 6 min and escalated to their user; gamma finally released its claim; `stuck` showed `stale_ask` only; no resolution | 20, 13 and 16 turns across the three members (the comment does not say which member had which), 0.0108, 0.0074 and 0.0092 USD | |

- **OBSERVED.** Two members, no rule: no deadlock when each simply answers (free).
- **OBSERVED.** An ask-level hold-and-wait cycle made of real claims produced an `ask_cycle` event with real agents, the `wait` output reported it, and the agents resolved the cycle themselves within one round (hold2, about 20 s).
- **OBSERVED.** When the claim step was only a suggestion (hold), the agents did not claim, no cycle existed for klatch to see, and neither agent broke the standoff by itself; both escalated to the user after about 6 minutes. Claim-taking depended on how strongly the prompt worded it (the comment's own caveat).
- **OBSERVED.** In the 3-member ring only one of the three asks was claimed, so no `ask_cycle` fired; only `stale_ask` events were produced and nothing resolved.
- **NOT SHOWN.** Any cycle among three members resolving (the ring had claims on one of three links, not all three); an agent interrupted mid-task by every ask in the lobby; a crashed holder mid-cycle. The cost of every member being helper to every other was not separated from these runs.
- Ambiguous in the comment: the per-member turns and costs of the ring (three numbers each, no member names); the comment gives the hold2 time as "about 20 s" and the hold time as "about 6 min" for the pair, not per agent. The ring's "about 6 min" is for everyone.
- Caveats, from the comment: the cycle was instructed by a team rule; haiku only; one run per variant. The ticket's "run at least twice" holds in total across the four variants. The ticket's done-when, a RESEARCH-NOTES record with OBSERVED and NOT OBSERVED lines, is this note; the comment said "RESEARCH-NOTES not edited" at the time.

### A cycle through asks (#273, comments 7068235230 and 7137775762)

Scenario: `alpha` and `beta` each need the other's port number (each port is "the other's plus 10"), neither knows either, both told not to guess or search. Each asks with `klatch ask`, waits (`wait --timeout 40`, up to 5 times) and finishes by telling the user the number or what it is blocked on. Three variants, one run each: plain (only the above), stuck (the prompt also says it may run `klatch stuck lobby` and `klatch asks lobby`), and filter (waits with `--only 'answer to'`). Turns and cost per agent, as the comment gives them:

| Variant | alpha | beta |
|---|---|---|
| plain | 9 turns, 0.0052 USD | 11 turns, 0.0059 USD |
| stuck | 11 turns, 0.0056 USD | 11 turns, 0.0062 USD |
| filter | 11 turns, 0.0059 USD | 9 turns, 0.0053 USD |

- **OBSERVED.** In all six sessions the agents noticed the loop within one wait round by reading the other's ask, said so in the room (several used `declined #N cannot:`), refused to guess, used their whole wait budget, then stopped, told their user they were blocked, named the circular dependency and asked the user for one real value. They did not loop forever and nobody invented a number. Even the `--only 'answer to'` filter did not hide the cycle, because the agents had already read the other's ask before filtering.
- **OBSERVED.** `klatch stuck lobby` at the end of every run showed `stale_ask` for ask #1 (asker alpha, age 178 s) and ask #2 (asker beta, age 178 s), and no `ask_cycle` event, because nobody claimed the other's ask and the rule (the design item 90) needs live ask claims.
- **Conclusion, as the closing comment (7137775762, 2026-11-24) states it:** the convention alone is enough for agents to see a cycle through asks, decline and escalate to the user, in this scenario. The same comment records that the detection rule is built (`proto/klatch/src/domain/ask_cycle.rs`, PR #791, ask_cycle events in `klatch stuck`, the notes in `klatch wait`). The raw run records live on the ticket and in the scratchpad, not in the repository.
- **NOT OBSERVED.** That the built `ask_cycle` rule fires or helps in the plain, stuck and filter runs (it did not fire; the cycle was through unclaimed asks). It was seen firing with real agents only in the #246 hold2 run above, where both agents had claimed. A helper agent present in the scenario; agents of a model other than haiku.
- Caveats, from the comment: the dependency was constructed by the prompt (each told it cannot know its number before the other's), haiku only, one run per variant, no helper agent present.
